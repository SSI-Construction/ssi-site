General contractors carry a unique burden: you're accountable for the budget, the schedule, and every trade on site — but you rarely control all three directly. Cost overruns, schedule slips, and communication breakdowns downstream of a subcontractor still land on your desk. That's why **construction project management for GCs** looks different from project management in almost any other industry.

This playbook covers the core responsibilities of GC project management, the pain points that come from doing it with spreadsheets, and what to look for in **construction management software** built for the job.

## The core responsibilities of a GC project manager

- **Budget oversight** — tracking committed costs (purchase orders) against actual spend (invoices) by cost code, in real time.
- **Schedule coordination** — sequencing every trade so work doesn't collide, and adjusting the critical path when one trade slips.
- **Trade management** — keeping subcontractors informed of look-ahead schedules, site conditions, and scope changes.
- **Owner and consultant reporting** — producing clean, current reports on demand, not just at monthly meetings.
- **Change and risk control** — logging every change order and its cost/schedule impact before work proceeds.

Doing all five well, at the same time, on every active project, is the actual job.

## Where spreadsheets break down

Spreadsheets work fine for a single project with a single owner. They break down fast once a GC is running several projects with dozens of cost codes, trades, and changes each:

- Budget and schedule live in separate files, so nothing updates automatically when a PO is issued or a task slips.
- Every report to an owner or lender is rebuilt by hand.
- Trade updates arrive by text, email, and phone call — and nothing is centralized.
- There's no single, current answer to "where do we actually stand on cost and schedule today?"

## What to look for in project management software for construction

A **project management software for construction** built for GCs should:

1. **Connect budget, schedule, POs, and invoices through shared cost codes** — one change updates every report.
2. **Support look-ahead schedules** that trades can see without a separate license.
3. **Generate owner and lender reports in one click**, not a rebuild every time.
4. **Track change orders** with cost and schedule impact, tied to an approval trail.
5. **Scale across unlimited projects** without per-project fees eating into margin.
