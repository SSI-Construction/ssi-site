Builders and trade subcontractors across Australia and New Zealand are entering one of the most challenging periods in recent years. While project pipelines are stabilising and infrastructure investment remains strong, margins are tightening, labour shortages persist, and cost pressures continue to shape feasibility. Recent industry reports show that builders are optimistic about future workloads, but far less confident about profitability.

In this environment, digital tools are no longer optional — they’re becoming essential for protecting margins, improving productivity, and keeping projects moving.

SSI Construction Manager and SSI Trade Manager were built specifically for small‑to‑midsize builders and trades who need simple, affordable software that improves project control without the complexity of enterprise systems.

## **The Pressure on Builders and Trades Is Increasing**

### **1. Cost pressures are reshaping profitability**

Across New Zealand and Australia, builders report rising labour costs, elevated material prices, and tighter tender competition. Even with strong pipelines, many firms expect margins to decline over the next 12 months.

### **2. Infrastructure work is booming — but requires discipline**

Australia’s infrastructure pipeline exceeds $240 billion, with major transport, energy, and water projects driving demand. This creates opportunity, but also requires stronger scheduling, cost control, and documentation.

### **3. Digital capability is becoming a baseline requirement**

Major projects increasingly expect builders and trades to use digital tools for:

* scheduling
* cost tracking
* compliance
* documentation
* real‑time reporting

Digital adoption is accelerating across the region.

### **4. Labour shortages demand better productivity**

Australia faces a shortage of more than 100,000 construction workers. Builders and trades must reduce admin time and maximise billable output.
